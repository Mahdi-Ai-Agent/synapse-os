# UI design

Synapse should feel like talking to a mind that keeps everything in its place, and like looking at that mind as a map: a deep-space canvas where every category of your life is a glowing region you can fly into. You talk on the chat page; you explore on the mind map; you look things up on the places page, where every category and island has a page of its own. Every item looks like what it is — a goal shows its progress, a habit its streak, a person your moments together, a saved reel its thumbnail.

## Visual language

- **Theme:** dark only. Background `#03050b`; surfaces are glass (`--glass-bg`/`--panel-bg` gradients, 22–26 px backdrop blur, 1 px periwinkle-tinted line, 14–22 px radius, soft drop shadow). One accent, periwinkle `#8f9fff` (with cyan `#7ee6f7` in gradients); success `#4ade9b`, warning `#fbbf24`, danger `#fb7185`. Tokens live in `styles/index.css` `@theme`.
- **Motion:** `motion` springs for panels, pages and chat messages; CSS for hovers and label fades; the map animates continuously (drift, pulses, nebulae). `prefers-reduced-motion` stops drift, pulses, flights and births and shortens every transition.

## Type

Three roles, set as CSS variables by `lib/fonts.ts` (DECISIONS 2026-10-31 "Fonts"):

| Role | Variable | Default | Free stand-in | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interface | `--font-sans` | IRANSans (`IRANSansX`) | Vazirmatn | menus, buttons, forms, cards |
| Headings | `--font-display` | Yekan Bakh | Estedad | page and panel titles, region names on the map, the chat greeting, numbers in widgets |
| Reading | `--font-reading` | Vazirmatn | — | chat messages and the composer, notes (Markdown and the editor), item names on the map |

Yekan Bakh and IRANSans are commercial: they are used only when installed on the computer (by family name), never bundled; Estedad and Vazirmatn (OFL, `@fontsource`) ship with the app. In English, Inter comes first in every stack. **Settings → Appearance** picks a font per role (stored per device in `localStorage`) and shows whether each commercial font is installed. Sizes: page titles 28 px/800, panel titles 21–23 px/800, body 13–15 px, chat text 14.5–15 px with 1.8–1.9 line height. Numerals follow the UI locale (Persian digits in fa; Persian comma in dates). **Clock:** every time of day is 24-hour (`14:05`, never AM/PM) — shown (`lib/format.ts` `fmtDate` with `hourCycle: 'h23'`), picked (`components/TimeField.tsx`: an hour 0–23 and minutes, replacing the browser's own time input, which follows the system's 12-hour setting) and written by the brain (its runtime context asks for it). Facts in one line are separated by `sep()` — «،» in Persian, where a middle dot beside digits reads as a zero; ` · ` in English.

## Words

Plain everyday words, the same ones the brain uses (vault AGENTS.md): an *item* (مورد), never a "node"; kinds by their common names — یادداشت، ایده، کار، پروژه، هدف، عادت، خاطره، آدم، لینک، روزنوشت، دسته (Note, Idea, Task, Project, Goal, Habit, Moment, Person, Link, Journal, Category). Statuses read like speech: a link is "for later / in progress / finished"; a goal is "active / achieved". Inbox filing is "Sort it". No type names, file paths or tool names in the UI except under "More details".

## Colour

Categories carry the colour; kinds carry the shape and icon (`graph/colors.ts`, OKLCH).

- **Hue per category:** a golden-angle sequence from 205° by the category's `order` (VAULT-SPEC "Categories"), so adding a category never repaints the others; an own colour (below) replaces it.
- **Family per category:** every member is a shade of its category's hue — lightness, chroma and a small hue shift by kind (area 0.80/0.17, project 0.78/0.17 −8°, goal 0.84/0.16 +4°, habit 0.80/0.14 −14°, event 0.86/0.10 +26°, task 0.83/0.13 +12°, idea 0.90/0.12 −22°, note 0.75/0.11 +20°, resource 0.72/0.13 +32°, person 0.82/0.09 −32°, ghost 0.58/0.035). Closed items lose light and chroma and are drawn hollow. Colours are clamped into sRGB.
- **Special groups:** Inbox (silver, centre of the map), Journal (sand), No category (slate) — low-chroma families.
- The signature colour of a family (L 0.80, C 0.15) is what chips, cards and panel headers use (`lib/categories.ts` `usePalette()`); nebulae use a deeper version (L 0.60, C 0.17). The add dialog's kind icons have fixed accent tints; they are UI hints, not item colours.
- **The owner's colour:** a category or a map can carry its own `color` (VAULT-SPEC), picked from the whole spectrum. It sets the family's tone — its hue, its vividness (measured against its hue's signature chroma, so the quick picks draw exactly like the automatic families) and its lightness (held to L 0.60–0.94, what the night map can show) — and every kind's shade above shifts with it (`colors.ts` `Tone`, `toneOfHex`, `familyRgb`). A map's colour is its island's: centre, haze, categories and items; a category of the island with a colour of its own keeps it (`model.ts` `familyTones`). Automatic (no `color`) goes back to the sequence, or for a category in a map to the map's colour.
- **The colour picker** (`components/ColorPicker.tsx`), from the category card on the map (its palette button), a category's or island's page, the category dialog and the map card: the whole spectrum as a field — hue across, vividness up — at the brightness its slider sets, twelve quick picks, the hex code (kept exactly when the map can show it; a too dark or too light code is brought into range) and Automatic. While the owner drags, the map, the chips and the cards recolour at once (`store.colorDraft`; `engine.setColors` when only colours changed — nothing is rebuilt and nothing moves); the colour is written to the vault when the pointer lets go, a quick pick is pressed or a code is entered.

## Shapes

One silhouette per kind, everywhere: the map draws it (`graph/shaders.ts` `shape()`) and `components/TypeIcon.tsx` draws the same silhouette as the icon in chips, cards, menus, the legend and the add dialog — a target means a goal and a soft triangle a moment on the map and on the page alike. Note circle · project hexagon · task rounded square · idea four-point star · person ring · link book · journal diamond · goal target · habit pentagon · moment soft triangle · category circle with an orbit · map circle with two orbits and its symbol on it · the mind map's core: the brain itself ("Living graph" → The brain) · inbox dashed circle · ghost dashed ring · tag small square. Progress is drawn into the shape: a goal's ring closes clockwise from the top as `progress` grows (the rest stays as a faint track); a project's hexagon fills from the bottom with the share of its tasks done. Finished items are drawn hollow; archived ones are off the map (see "Done and archived").

## Shell

```
 RTL (Persian)                                                   LTR mirrors it
┌────────────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────┬───────────┐
│ item panel (end side, when an item is open) │      PAGE        │  Sidebar  │
│                                             │ chat · map · … │ (start)   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────┴───────────┘
```

- **Sidebar** (start edge: right in Persian, left in English; 256 px, folds to a 68 px icon column, remembered per device): logo, **New chat**, the pages — Chat · Mind map · Guide · Goals · Plan · Inbox (with its count as a badge) · Me · Categories & islands (see "Places") — then Settings (a dot when the brain needs attention), the language switch and the theme switch (light ⇄ dark, see "Light theme"). Past chats live inside the chat page (see "Chat").
- **Pages:** one at a time in the rest of the window. The mind map stays mounted behind the others (paused while hidden) so its camera and layout survive navigation. The last page is remembered per device; a fresh install opens the chat.
- **Item panel:** opening an item from a page (a card, a search hit) slides its details in on the end side (400 px), beside the page; on the map they open on the start side instead (see "Mind map").
- **Toasts:** bottom centre, with an optional action (Undo after a delete, "Chat" after handing something to the brain).

## Chat

The home page, laid out like ChatGPT or Claude (`views/ChatView.tsx`): the chat in one centred column (780 px), with the past chats in a column of their own on the start side.

- **One conversation:** the open conversation stays the same while you move between pages and is remembered on this device, so the app opens on it again; only **New chat** (here or in the map's conversation panel) or picking another chat changes it (the user's call, 2026-11-02: "switching to a new conversation is my choice").
- **Chats column** (264 px, folds from the header's history button, remembered per device; a full-screen sheet on the phone): its own **New chat**, then the past chats grouped Today · Yesterday · Last 7 days · Older, each with its time and message count; the open one is highlighted. Each row has a ⋯ menu (on hover, on the open chat, always on touch screens): **Rename** turns the row into a field (Enter saves, Escape cancels) and **Delete** asks once more in place ("Press again to delete"); deleting the open chat starts a new one; a chat the brain is still answering in cannot be deleted (`PATCH` / `DELETE /api/threads/:id`).
- **Empty chat:** the brain orb, the greeting ("What is on your mind today?") and one line on what to say, the composer in the middle of the page, and four example cards under it (keep an idea · save a link for later · remember a moment · ask your mind) that fill the composer.
- **Composer:** a large rounded box; Enter sends, Shift+Enter breaks the line; `+` opens **Add** (by hand), ⚡ saves the text to the inbox without the brain; the send button turns into Stop while the brain works. **Voice:** a microphone beside the send button (and in the map's tell bar) — press, speak, press again; what is said appears in the box as it is recognized, after what was typed, in the UI language (`lib/dictation.ts`: the browser's own speech recognition — Chrome sends the audio to Google's speech service, Safari to Apple's; the dictation of Claude Code and of the ChatGPT app belongs to those apps and is not open to others); it stops when the message is sent, says in plain words when the microphone is not allowed or the service cannot be reached, and is hidden where the browser has none and in the Mac app (ARCHITECTURE "Desktop app" → Voice). A three-way switch in its bar says what a message does: **Chat** (the brain files what is worth keeping and answers questions; when the category, the kind or a needed fact is unclear it asks first — its best guess and at most two questions — and files on the answer in the same chat), **Consult** (the `advise` skill: the brain thinks a worry, a decision or a knot through from what the vault knows — goals, projects, people, recent days — asks what is missing, answers with what it sees, the options, its take and one next step, and changes nothing unless asked) or **Make** (the `make` skill: the brain writes something — a channel post, a caption, a message to someone, a bio — in the user's voice, from the map it is for and the maps connected to it, the character map carrying the voice and values; it replies with the piece ready to paste and saves nothing unless asked). Consult and make have their own greeting, hint and example cards and their own orb tint (amber, green). The map's tell bar has no switch: the brain reads the message itself — a worry is consult, "write me a post" is make, the rest is chat. Beside the switch (and in the tell bar) the **scope pick** appears whenever a map is cut off from the mind map (see "Scopes").
- **Header:** the past-chats toggle, the chat's title, the **brain picker** and New chat. The picker (`components/EnginePicker.tsx`) chooses, for this device and every run — chat, consult, reviews, the insight — the engine among those that can run now (signed in, or holding an accepted key), then its model and effort; the choice is remembered per device, the defaults live in Settings, and a thread that switches engines starts a new engine session.
- **Messages:** read like a messenger (the user's call, 2026-11-01): the user's in a soft periwinkle bubble on the right, the brain's in a lighter neutral bubble on the left — in both directions, each bubble's tail on its own side, the brain's orb beside its bubble; the brain's as Markdown with clickable `[[links]]` beside its orb, with copy, time and spend on hover. While it works: the brain at work — its mark breathing while a signal runs out along a synapse and back (`components/BrainWorking.tsx`) — with "Thinking…" and the step count, or "Queued · n ahead" only when runs are really ahead; text streams in. The map's tell bar shows the same mark.
- **What it saved:** under a reply, "n saved · n updated" with **Show on the map** and **Undo** (reverts the run's commit), then the items themselves as their kinds' cards (see "Kinds"); a click opens the item. Items that no longer exist are named as such. Runs started elsewhere (reviews, inbox sorting) show their label (`display`) instead of the app-written prompt.
- A review from Today starts a fresh chat and opens it; inbox sorting runs in its own chat, reachable from the inbox row.

## Kinds

Each kind has a card (`kinds/Cards.tsx`) and a detail view (`kinds/Detail.tsx`); every control saves on its own (kind fields through `facets`, VAULT-SPEC "Kinds"), so there is no form and no Save button.

| Kind | Card | Details: chips in the header · the kind's block |
|---|---|---|
| Task | round check (toggles done), title, summary, due date (red when late) | state (to do · doing · done · dropped), due, importance, project · big "Mark done" |
| Project | progress bar (done/total tasks), status, next step | state (active · paused · done · archived), deadline, tasks done · progress ring, its tasks with an inline "Add a task" |
| Goal | progress ring, why (or summary), target date | state (active · achieved · paused · dropped), by when, progress · ring + slider, why it matters, what moves it forward (linked projects, habits, tasks) |
| Habit | today's check-in, cadence · time, streak 🔥, the last 7 days as dots (today toggles) | state (active · paused · archived), how often, time of day, streak · big "I did it today", streak, four weeks of dots |
| Moment (event) | date tile, mood emoji, place, the people's avatars | when, where, mood (five faces) · with whom |
| Person | avatar, relation, birthday soon, number of moments | relation, birthday, contact · avatar, how you met, moments together |
| Link (resource) | the preview image (Open Graph / oEmbed via `/api/preview`) or a platform-coloured tile, platform · kind badge, play mark for videos and reels (portrait for reels), status | state (for later · in progress · finished · archived), what it is, platform · large preview, Open, the link |
| Journal | weekday and date, mood, summary | day, mood |
| Idea · note | icon, title, summary | project |

Cards come in two sizes: full (grids on a place's page) and compact (chat replies, the place card, Today). Finished and archived items fade (see "Done and archived").

## Item details

`components/NodePanel.tsx`, in the item panel, on the map's start side or as a phone sheet.

What a thing *is* comes first, then what it says — in one card — then the thing itself.

- **Header** (washed in the category's colour): a bar with back (when opened from a category card), ⋯ (show on the map · edit the details · copy the title · archive / take out of the archive for kinds without a state chip · delete) and close; then one **facts row**: the **kind badge** first — the kind's glyph in the item's colour and its everyday name; a click opens a menu of the nine kinds with their one-line hints, and picking one turns the item into that kind (its file moves folders, VAULT-SPEC "Kinds") — then the **category** chip (pick another or none; an inferred category is marked "guessed" with **Keep it**), the **state** chip (its menu lists the kind's states with what each one means; a finished item's chip reads "Done · 8 Nov"), the kind's chips from the table above, and an "Archived" chip when it is. Every chip edits itself — a menu, the native date picker (dates clear with a small ×), a small box for short text — and saves at once; an empty fact shows as a dashed placeholder ("No due date", "Where was it?").
- **The card:** one bordered box in the category's colour holding the **title** and the one-line **summary** (both edited in place: click, type, Enter; a new title renames the item and rewrites its links, VAULT-SPEC "Renames"), then **Details** with its edit button beside the label on the start side (**Edit the details** / **Write** opens the TipTap editor — an unchanged body is written back byte-identical; `[[Title]]` makes a link), and under the text the item's **history**: a timeline of the day it was created, every dated line in its body (`- YYYY-MM-DD: what changed`, VAULT-SPEC "Node files" → History — the brain appends one whenever it updates the item from conversation) and the day it was finished. The dated lines are lifted out of the rendered text (`lib/history.ts`).
- **Body:** the kind's block (the ring and slider, the check-in and the weeks, the preview, the people, the moments), **Connected to** (links both ways that the kind view does not already show), **Maybe related** (lexical suggestions with Connect / Dismiss).
- **More** (collapsed): tags, other names, people, created, updated, finished (when it is), the file path.
- A category opened as an item shows its counts by kind and See everything · Show on the map · Add here.

## Adding things

`components/Dialogs.tsx` "Add" (from the composer's `+`, a category, or the map's place card):

1. **What are you adding?** — nine kinds, each with a one-line hint — or **just write it**: a sentence handed to the brain, which decides the kind and the category (the reply is in the chat).
2. A short form with only that kind's fields: task (due, project), project (deadline), goal (by when, why), habit (how often, time of day), moment (when — today by default — mood, with whom), person (relation, birthday), link (paste it: the title fills from its preview), plus a few more words and the category → `POST /api/nodes` (`facets` for kind fields). The new item opens and the map flies to it.

Categories: **New category / Edit** (name, one-line summary, colour: automatic or one of twelve swatches); **Delete** says how many items it holds and asks where they go (another category or none) → `POST /api/categories/:id/delete`; item **Delete** → `DELETE /api/nodes/:id`. Both toast with Undo (reverts the commit).

## Places

`views/PlacesView.tsx` (the sidebar's **Categories & islands**; its pure parts in `lib/places.ts`): first every place, then each one as a page of its own.

- **The list:** two headings, **Categories** (the mind map's) and **Islands**, each a grid of cards that carry the place's name and nothing else — its colour as a soft glow and border, an island's symbol beside its name. **New island** and **New category** sit at the top.
- **A place's page** (a category, an island or the mind map): a back link, then a quiet **hero** — the orb or the island's symbol, the name and its line (both edited in place), one line of counts (categories, items, urgent), the colour picker and ⋯ (a category: move it to the mind map or to an island, delete it; an island: a new category in it), **Add to this category** and **Show on the map** — then four tabs:
  - **Everything** — the place from the whole to its parts (`treeOf`): its larger items as big cards holding what they hold — a goal with the work linked to it, a project with its tasks, a person with the moments and notes that mention them — each child a row with its tick (see "Guide"); then everything else by kind as small cards; the retired folded at the end. An island or the mind map shows each of its categories this way, one section each (the first three open), with **Open** to go to that category's page.
  - **Map** (`components/PlaceGraph.tsx`) — the mind map's living graph drawn for this place alone: its centre (the category, or the island's centre and its categories), its items that are not done or retired, and the links among them, in a framed canvas of its own; a click opens an item in the panel. It lays itself out afresh and saves nothing, so the mind map's own layout is never touched.
  - **Needs attention** — what to act on here in the brain's order, with ticks (`ActionsByPlace`, see "Guide").
  - **Analysis** (a category, an island): the brain's look at this place alone — In short, Moving, Stuck, Patterns, Next steps, with its date — and **Analyze it** / **Analyze again**. The button sends `/insight [[Place]] → memory/places/<file>.md`, the file named by the app (`GET /api/places/:id/analysis` → `{path, text}`), so the brain never works out a file name. The run gets a chat of its own and leaves the open conversation alone; its end is toasted in every open copy ("The analysis of … is ready", with See; a copy that did not start it finds the place in the run's prompt) unless that place's page is on screen.
- On the phone the list comes first and a place opens with a back link.

## Mind map

The map page (`components/GraphView.tsx`), laid out like a map app:

- **Search box** (top, start side; `/` or ⌘K): typing dims non-matches and lists up to 8 hits with their category; arrows move, Enter or a click flies to the hit and opens it, Escape clears.
- **Category chips** (top, beside the search): one per region in its colour with its item count; a click opens that category (as a click on the region does), a second click closes it; specials after the categories; "+ New category" at the end.
- **Place panel** (start side under the search box, 388 px): a click on a category — its name on the map, its centre, a chip, or (zoomed out) anywhere in its cloud — **flies the camera into the region** (an eased flight with a slight crane lift, framed in the free area) and opens the **category card**: name and line (edited in place), its colour (the palette button, see "Colour"), "n items", **Add here** and **See everything**, then its items by kind as compact cards (four per kind, "and n more"); the other regions fade. A click on an item (on the map or in the card) opens its details in the same panel with a back arrow to the card. **Stepping back:** a click on empty map, Escape, the back arrow or ✕ goes one step back — item → card → nothing — and the camera flies back to where it was before that step (the category framing, then the overview), unless the user panned or zoomed by hand in between (`engine.ts` `cameraPose` / `movedSince`; `GraphView` remembers the camera's goal before every way in).
- **Toolbar** (end side, bottom): show everything · zoom in · zoom out · flat/3D · **names** (three steps — every name, items' names off, no names at all — remembered per device; see "Map-style labels") · **spread** and **gather** (the two brush tools, see "Spreading") · **Maps** (see "Maps") · what to show (the kinds with their glyphs and counts — the legend of shapes — finished items, archived items, unconnected only, tags on the map) · time-lapse · **Re-arrange** (confirm → lay everything out again and forget saved places).
- **Tell bar** (bottom centre): "Tell or ask your brain…"; a message goes on the open conversation, whose messages and streaming reply float in the **conversation panel** (`components/graph/MapChat.tsx`) while new items fly into their regions. The panel opens above the bar; drag it anywhere on the map by its header, resize it from its bottom corner on the end side (bottom-left in Persian), and **Back to its first place and size** returns it — it stays whole on the map (`lib/floating.ts`), and its place and whether it is open are kept per device, across pages and restarts. Its buttons: **New conversation** (the only way the map starts another one), **Continue in chat**, Hide; while it is hidden a button on the bar shows it again. On a phone it stays docked above the bar.
- **Hover card:** kind, category, title, summary, due date; on a category's centre, "drag to move the whole region".
- **Item menu** (right-click; long-press on touch): Open · Focus on this · Show its category · Mark done / Reached it · Archive · Open it again / Take out of the archive · Ask the brain to connect it · Let it float again (after a drag) · Copy the title · Delete.
- **Keyboard** (map page only, never while typing): arrows pan, `+`/`-` zoom, `f` fits, Escape clears the menu → search → one step back (a menu or popover that closes on Escape keeps the key).
- **Dragging:** dragging an item pins it where it is dropped; **dragging a category's centre moves its whole region** (its anchor and every item in it). Where things are — every item's place, the region anchors, pins — is saved to `.synapse/layout.json` (`PUT /api/layout`, debounced, after a drag and whenever the layout settles; flat view only) and restored before the first layout on the next load, on any device; every other open copy of the app — another tab, the phone — takes a save at once, and a copy saves only what changed on its own map, so none puts an older map back (ARCHITECTURE "Layout").
- **Spreading:** two brush tools in the toolbar. The **spread** button turns the cursor into the spreading mark (four arrows outward) with a dashed brush ring around it (110 px, 140 px on touch screens) and a hint under the chips; while it is on hover stays quiet, a drag still pans, and **every click opens up the crowded spot under the brush a little**: each item there gets more room (`graph/layout.ts` `spread`, up to four times its own space, most at the brush's centre) — a wider personal space, a stronger push on its neighbours, longer links, a looser pull to its region's centre — plus a push away from the click, and the region settles open and stays so. The **gather** button is its opposite (arrows inward, its own hint): **every click pulls the items under the brush closer together**, past the spacing the map was laid out with, down to 0.4 of it (`ROOM_MIN`; items never overlap). Shift-click (or Alt-click) does the other tool's work while held. Both stay where the owner left them: the room is saved with the layout (`room` in `.synapse/layout.json`) at the click itself and again once the map has settled, a save still waiting goes out when the page is hidden, reloaded or closed and when the map is left, and a map saved mid-move settles fully on the next load (`settled`); another open copy of the app follows at once and cannot put the old spacing back; only the other tool or **Re-arrange** undoes it. Escape, the button or the hint's ✕ ends the tool; pinned items keep their place.

## Maps

The mind map is everything; beside it the user keeps **maps** (VAULT-SPEC "Maps"): the **character map** the brain builds about them, and custom maps for a business, a page, a long piece of work. On the canvas each map is an **island** drawn in the language of the rest of the map — one colour family and a centre, like a category:

- **One colour per island.** A map has a hue (gold for the character map, a warm sequence for the others, `colors.ts` `zoneHue`) or the colour its owner picked (the map card's **colour**), and every region and item of the island is a shade of it — a category with a colour of its own excepted — exactly as a category's members are shades of the category's hue (`graph/model.ts`, `lib/categories.ts`); the island's regions' nebulae and the chips of its categories share it. Under the regions lies a mellow haze in the same hue — a wide soft field whose reach wobbles slowly so it is never a circle, broken up by the nebulae's cloud texture, with no edge and no ring (`shaders.ts` `ZONE_FRAGMENT`).
- **A centre.** The map node itself is drawn at the island's heart — the empty middle of its ring of categories (`layout.ts` `computeRing` centre group `map:<id>`) — as a hub like a category's centre: the same glassy circle with a slowly turning orbit and a second counter-turning one (shape `map`), with the map's **symbol** drawn on it (a minimal line icon from core `MAP_SYMBOLS` and `graph/zoneGlyphs.ts`: a figure for the character map, a star by default, or a briefcase, store, camera, globe, book, heart, music note or code mark the user picks in the New map dialog or the map card). Its name and item count hang under it at every zoom; from afar that name is all the island says — its regions' names come in as the island opens on screen (about a third of the view), item names closer still, the mind map's own levels. The centre is the island's handle: a click opens the map's card and frames the island, remembering where the camera was like a category click does, so an empty-map click or Escape glides back; **dragging it carries the whole island** — every region of the map — like dragging a category's centre carries its region (saved with the layout); its hover card reads "Map · name · purpose".
- **The switch.** Beside every island sits its **toggle**: a small iOS-like switch in the island's colour with no words (`labels.ts` `updateToggles`), set permanently just past the island's outermost node on its **open side** — the side that faces away from everything else on screen — upright on the island's left or right, flat above or below it. Lit, with the knob at the lit end, while the map is linked to the mind map; dark otherwise. One press writes the switch to the vault — off cuts the map off from everything (the mind map and every other map) without forgetting a connection (`apart: true`, VAULT-SPEC "Maps"); on brings every connection back as it was, and links the map to the mind map if it never was — and the island stays exactly where it is.
- **The core.** The mind map has a centre too: the **core** node (`core:mind`, client-side, periwinkle), drawn as the brain itself ("Living graph" → The brain) and there as soon as the vault holds anything, even before the mind map has a category of its own; it sits at the centre of the mind map's ring where the inbox used to sit (the inbox is a region like the others now; a layout saved before the core existed leaves the inbox's old place at the heart); a click frames the mind map (an empty-map click or Escape brings the camera back), and dragging it carries the whole mind map, as a map's centre carries its island.
- **Every relation drawn.** Links inside a region and between regions are the vault's own; the structure is drawn on top (`GraphView` `withStructure`): the core to each of the mind map's categories, each map's centre to its categories (the vault's own `map:` relation), a map's centre to the core while it is connected to the mind map, and a line between the centres of two connected maps. Connected maps' cross links are drawn as **bridges** — brighter, lifted toward white along their whole length; unconnected maps' cross links are neither drawn nor pulling. A map that has never been placed is set down near the mind map when connected to it and far out when not; from then on its island keeps its place through every connection change (the links simply appear or go), and dragging an island's region keeps it there like any region.

- **Maps panel** (toolbar, `components/graph/MapsPanel.tsx`): the mind map with its count; each map with its count, a show/hide switch (per device), its **Connected to** chips — the mind map and every other map, each a toggle written to the vault (`lib/maps.ts` `connectionPatches`); a connection kept while one of its islands is switched off is drawn dashed, and pressing it switches both back on — and, for the character map, **Refresh it** (**Build it** when there is none yet); **New map** at the end.
- **Map card** (`NodePanel` → `MapSummary`, from the island's centre, the panel or the Me tab): the kind badge "Map", its name and purpose (edited in place), the same connection chips with a line on what connecting means, the **symbol** picker (`SymbolPicker`, saved as the `symbol` field), the island's **colour** (the colour picker, saved as `color`), its categories (a click opens the category on the map), **Show on the map** (frames the island), **New category here** (a category made from here carries `map`), and for the character map Build / Refresh.
- **New map** (`Dialogs.tsx` `MapForm`): name, what it is for, its symbol, connected to the mind map and to the character map by default; the map opens on the canvas and the user tells the chat what goes in it (the brain files into a map's own categories, creating them inside the map, never into the mind map's).
- **Character map:** built by `/character` from the whole vault (traits, values, concerns, voice — each item with its evidence), rebuilt from the Maps panel, its card or the coach's **Me** tab (a card with its counts, Build / Refresh and Show on the map); a toast with **See** when a build finishes elsewhere. While it is connected to the mind map, consult and make runs write with the user's voice and values.
- Items of a hidden map leave the canvas; the places page and the panel still show them.

## Scopes

Connections decide what the brain can reach in a run. While every map is connected to the mind map — directly or through another map — there is one **core** and nothing to choose. The moment a map (or a group of maps linked only to each other) is cut off from the mind map, it becomes a **scope** of its own, and a **scope pick** appears beside the composer's mode switch and in the map's tell bar (`components/ScopePick.tsx`, `lib/maps.ts` `scopesOf`, mirrored by core `scopesOf` for the server): **The core** — the mind map and the maps connected to it, listed in the hint — or one cut-off group, named by its maps. The choice (per device, this session) goes with every run as `scope` (`POST /api/brain/run`); the server writes a **Scope** line into the runtime context (BRAIN-ENGINES "Modes") and the brain reads, files, answers and makes only inside it — a consult from the character map alone, a post from the page map alone, or the whole core. Reconnecting a map that was the chosen scope falls back to the core. With one core the run carries no scope and the brain decides for itself which space a message belongs to — a map's categories for what is about that map, the character map for what the user says about themselves, the mind map for the rest, several of them from one message when it says several things.

## Map-style labels

Names appear the way a map shows country, city and street names (`graph/labels.ts`, `engine.ts` `updateLabels`): what is shown depends on the on-screen gap between neighbouring items — the camera's pixels per world unit times the median nearest-neighbour distance of the layout:

| Gap | Shown |
|---|---|
| < 26 px | only **region names**: the category name in the heading font, "n items" under it, just above its cluster (below it when "above" would cover another region); clickable, they underline on hover |
| 26–42 px | region names fade out; category centres get their names |
| ≥ 38 px | projects, goals and well-connected items |
| ≥ 64 px | every item |

At every level labels are placed by priority (hovered, selected, focused and search hits always; then what was shown last frame, so panning does not flicker; then rank) below their item, else above it, else skipped — they never overlap — and never in the band under the search box and chips. Each label is keyed by its item and fades in and out (240 ms). The toolbar's **names** switch steps through three settings (per device): every name; items' names off — no item is named at any zoom while region and island names stay, the map's places; and no names at all — regions and islands lose theirs too, and the map is read by its colours, shapes and symbols (an island still moves by its centre). The hover card still names what is under the pointer. A category flight ends close enough for its items' names (≤ the 64 px level when the region fits); selecting an item from a card or a search hit brings the camera just close enough to read its neighbours.

## Living graph

The renderer under the map (`src/graph/`, DECISIONS 2026-10-31): `layout.ts` (d3-force-3d), `engine.ts` (scene, input, loop), `camera.ts`, `labels.ts`, `shaders.ts`, `model.ts` (data → drawables).

- **Regions:** each category (and each special group) has an anchor: the mind map's core at the centre (the inbox, before a vault has any category), the others evenly on a ring sized so neighbouring regions never overlap (a Fibonacci sphere in 3D); a region keeps its anchor once it has one — a new category finds a free slot, a grown one stays, a refresh that changes nothing stirs nothing — and the layout is saved after every change (`/api/layout`), so the map comes back exactly as it was left; a region the user placed keeps its anchor, and a new category's ring slot walks outward until it clears placed regions. Every item is pulled toward its anchor (the area, the region's centre, strongly); links inside a region are short and firm, links between regions long and weak; repulsion is local (170 units). The first layout is seeded from ids, so it looks the same on every load; a saved layout settles gently from where it was; a new item starts beside a linked item or inside its region.
- **Nebula:** one additive quad per region at its smoothed centroid, sized from its spread, filled with a slowly drifting cloud sampled from a baked tileable fbm texture (`bakeNoise`), in the region's deep colour. It fades as it grows on screen; the open region brightens and the others fade.
- **Items:** one instanced draw call of camera-facing quads; a signed-distance shader draws the shape per kind — note circle, project hexagon, task rounded square, idea four-point star, person ring, link book-shaped rectangle, journal diamond, goal target (ring and centre), habit pentagon, moment soft triangle, area large circle with a slowly turning three-gap orbit, inbox circle with a dashed waiting ring, ghost dashed ring, tag small square — with glassy shading, a halo that shrinks for tiny far items, and rings: white for selected, expanding green for items the brain just created, blue for updated, an outer ring for the focus. Size grows with degree. An item never drops below 2.6 px.
- **Links:** two halves per link (fat lines, 1.25 px, additive): inside a region in its colour; between regions a gradient from one family to the other that fades in the middle, dimmer still for journal and inbox links.
- **Life:** every item drifts on its own slow Lissajous path; centres and the inbox breathe; signal pulses travel along lit links; new items grow in with an elastic pop and a burst of pulses; a 1600-star field with parallax sits far behind.
- **Ignition:** whenever the map comes on screen — the first load, and every return from another page or a refresh — it starts dark and lights up (`engine.ts` `playIntro`): each region's centre first, then its items in waves outward (link hops, with a little jitter), every item growing in with the elastic pop; as each item ignites it sends a signal down every link to an item that ignites after it, random traffic runs five times denser for the duration, the nebulae and the islands' outlines fade in from nothing, and the bloom swells and settles; the islands light up half a second after the mind map so their bridges go last. Around three seconds in all, and a data refresh mid-way (a saved layout settling, settings arriving) does not cut it short: the wave is keyed by item id, not by draw order. On a return the view also settles in from slightly farther away (×1.18 → 1 over 1.5 s) without moving where the user left the camera. Off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **The brain:** the core is drawn as a brain, not a hub (`graph/brain.ts`, `shaders.ts` `BRAIN_FRAGMENT`): a side view — the cerebrum's lobes, the cerebellum with its folia, the stem — in dark periwinkle glass whose gyri are rounded forms and whose sulci are thin grooves lit from inside (a labyrinth baked once while the browser is idle, `bakeFolds`), with a glass rim and a soft halo. At rest it breathes: the grooves hum, a neuron sparks now and then, a slow wave crosses the cortex every few seconds. It is never narrower on screen than about 48 px, so it reads as a brain from afar; the folds come in as it grows on screen and the folia closer still. Items keep clear of it (the core's radius is the brain's) and region names do not sit on it.
- **The brain at work:** while a run is live it thinks (`uWork`, `engine.setWorking`): the grooves glow brighter, waves of light run through the folds from where a thought starts, sparks fire everywhere, short bolts leap between its regions and aura waves leave its outline. Every file the brain reads or writes becomes a **reach** (`engine.signal`, `graph/arcs.ts`): a bolt runs from the brain's heart to its edge facing the item; an electric current — a jagged bolt that crackles, with charge running along it — runs hop by hop along the drawn links, through the item's own category first, and the category it passes flickers; the item flashes with a ring of light as the current arrives; the link stays charged while it is read (a little longer for a write); and the answer travels back as a packet in the item's colour, enters the brain and flashes in it. The index and the memory files are the brain's own, so reading them makes it think — a wave and a discharge — instead of reaching; an item nothing links to the core only flashes. The signals come from the run's tool events (`brain.event` `touched`, `lib/activity.ts`), so they are what the brain really touched, not a guess: Claude Code's and the API engines' file tools by their path; Codex's shell commands by the vault paths they name and its edits by their files (server `touchedBy`, `commandPaths`); a search across the whole vault makes the brain think; a new item the brain writes gets its reach once it appears on the map (`TouchMap`). Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the brain is still and there are no reaches.
- **Bloom:** `UnrealBloomPass` (threshold 0.32), stronger when close (0.30 → 0.60); disabled automatically after sustained slow frames. Pixel ratio capped at 1.5.
- **Camera:** perspective, eased toward its goal; pan by dragging the background (with inertia), zoom with the wheel or pinch toward the cursor, double-click empty space to zoom in; a small pointer-driven tilt adds parallax. The camera centres in the area the place panel leaves free (view offset). The first load flies in from far away, and frames the map again once the layout has settled (a slow machine cuts the warm-up short) unless the user has moved the camera by then. **3D mode** spreads the layout in depth and lets the drag orbit (Shift-drag pans); regions are not dragged or saved there.
- **Dimming:** hover lights the item and its neighbours; search hits, local focus and the open category dim everything else the same way.
- **Performance target:** ≥ 50 fps at 1440×900 on an Apple M1 at retina resolution (measured 60 fps zoomed out and zoomed in, 52 fps during a category flight, 60 fps while the brain works with 46 currents live, sample vault, headless Chrome on the GPU).

## Done and archived

Two ways out, with different meanings (VAULT-SPEC "Status"):

- **Finished** — `done` (task, project, link) and `achieved` (goal) — is history and stays, out of the way: on the map it leaves its category at once for the **archive corner** (below), so a category shows only what is still going on ("Show finished items" in the filters hides it there too); on a place's page it stays under its project or goal, ticked and struck through; it shows on the plan page under **Finished this week** for seven days, and the brain reads it for 30 days, and keeps the day it was finished (`completed`, read in its state chip). A task's check, "Reached it", the state chip and the brain (when the user says they did it) all finish an item the same way.
- **Archived** — `archived` (project, habit, link, note, idea, person, moment, category) and `dropped` (task, goal) — is out of the way. **Retired** items (archived, dropped, or finished 30+ days ago; VAULT-SPEC "Finished vs. archived") leave their categories on the map for the **archive corner**: one dim grey region past the bottom-left of everything else (`graph/layout.ts` `ARCHIVE_GROUP`), switched off — no line runs from it to anything live — and shown by default ("Show archived items" in the filters hides it); they are also out of the brain's daily index (`index/archive.md`) and out of what needs you. Archived items stay under **Archived** at the end of their category. Kinds with a state chip archive from it; the others from the ⋯ menu and the map's item menu — **Archive (off the map)** / **Back on the map** — which is how a person, a moment or a note that no longer weighs on the owner leaves the map by hand; the same menus open finished items again.

The state chip's menu says what each state means for that kind, so the difference is read where it is chosen. Reopening clears `completed`.

## i18n, RTL and mixed text

- Dictionaries `src/i18n/fa.ts` and `src/i18n/en.ts` with identical keys; `document.documentElement.dir`/`lang` follow the locale; radix primitives get the direction from `Direction.Provider`. Start/end mirror everything: in Persian the sidebar and the map's place panel sit on the right, item panels beside pages on the left, the user's chat bubbles on the left.
- The UI direction decides layout and alignment; user text never does. Titles and short text sit in `<bdi>`; text that truncates or wraps carries `dir="auto"` itself with the `.utext` class, so it keeps its own direction (the ellipsis lands at its end) while aligning to the UI side. Long-form content takes each block's own direction: in rendered Markdown and in the editor every paragraph, list item and heading gets the direction of the script most of its letters are in (`lib/format.ts` `dominantDir`; `Markdown.tsx`, the editor's `BlockDirection` plugin), so a Persian paragraph that opens with a Latin word stays right-to-left; the composer and chat bubbles use `dir="auto"`. Directional icons (back, next) and range sliders mirror with the locale; a habit's days run right to left in Persian. Latin tokens with punctuation inside Persian strings are wrapped in isolate marks. Every text field takes the UI's direction while it is empty and its text's own direction once typed (`lib/format.ts` `inputDir`): a browser resolves `dir="auto"` on an empty field to left-to-right, which put the placeholder and the caret on the wrong side in Persian.

## Accessibility

Every control is a real button or input with a label (icon buttons have `aria-label` and a tooltip); cards are `role="button"` and open on Enter; dialogs and menus trap focus and close on Escape (radix); colour is never the only signal (kind is also a shape and an icon; status is text); reduced motion is honoured.
